# Two looks: grown and built

Date: 2026-09-23 · Status: accepted

## Context

The field grew organic: edges that wave, stripes that lean, spots that wobble and are pushed apart until they fit
(see [organic but deterministic](2026-09-22-organic-but-deterministic.md)). It reads as something alive, which is
exactly right for some people and exactly wrong for others — the same instrument can be asked to look like a tool.

## Decision

One setting, two looks, the same geometry underneath.

_Grown_ is what it was: waved edges, a tilt that fans out, spots laid out with a little noise and separated by
repulsion, colours on a wide arc.

_Built_ takes everything irregular out. Stripes are upright bars with one even radius, centred on a line, no waves, no
tilt. The map drops into three columns and fixed rows. The palette narrows to graphite with one warm accent, and the
nudge of hue per pitch class disappears. Left and right speak the same language in both looks.

Nothing about what the field means changes: width still carries fitness, the vertical is still pull, the tonic still
has its seam.

## Why

The organic look explains itself — it invites touching. The built look is easier to read at a glance and looks like
equipment rather than a toy. Which of those a person wants is not a question with one answer, and it costs one flag
in the geometry and a branch in the drawing to offer both.

Keeping the meaning identical is the point: this is a skin, not a second instrument.

## Rejected

_Only the organic look._ It was read as playful to the point of imprecise by people who wanted to work with it.

_Only the built look._ It loses what makes the field feel like an instrument instead of a control panel.

_A slider between the two._ Halfway is neither, and every value in between would need its own design decisions.
