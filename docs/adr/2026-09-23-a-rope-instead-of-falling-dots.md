# A rope instead of falling dots

Date: 2026-09-23 · Status: accepted, supersedes the learn dots of 2026-09-21-learning-by-premonition

## Context

The learning mode showed the tone that was due as a dot on its stripe, with rings around it for repeats and paler
dots for what came next. It worked, and it taught nothing about the song: every tone was an event of its own, and
the thing that makes a melody a melody – that this tone goes to that one – was never on the screen. A row of
independent dots is a timeline, and a timeline is what a player has to translate back into music.

## Decision

The way through a song is one rope.

It begins where the hand is, at the height a hand plays at, and climbs. Every tone is a knot on it, every leg as long
as the tone lasts – and as thick: a whole note is a heavy piece of rope, a quaver a thin one. A leap between two
far-apart stripes is a steep diagonal, a repeated tone a straight climb upward. The shape of the melody is on the
screen before it is played.

The rope is braided rather than drawn as a line: two strands winding around a body, in the warm counterpart of
whatever the field is lacquered in – amber on graphite, the same amber against the polished blue. It is the one thing
on the field that is not made of the field's own language, because it does not belong to the instrument but to the
song.

The stripe of the tone that is due lights up along its whole length in the same colour: the key to press, not a mark
on it. Further along, the rope fades out, and fades back in as it comes closer – the far future is there without
demanding anything. When a tone is hit, the rope slides down by one leg instead of jumping.

A song brings its own chords, note by note: the field follows them where the rope arrives, and the band, the radio
and the auto-harmony step aside for the duration. Nothing accompanies a song but the hand.

## Why

Anticipation is the whole of playing by ear: you see where you are going and lean into it. A path shows that; a
series of dots asks you to reconstruct it.

Thickness for duration puts the rhythm into the same picture as the pitch, where it belongs – without a second
notation to read.

## Rejected

_Falling dots, as in the games._ They turn a melody into reflexes: everything arrives, nothing connects.

_Notes on a staff over the field._ A second notation, with its own rules, over an instrument built to need none.

_A rope with a constant thickness._ It would leave the rhythm to be guessed, and the picture would say that every
tone weighs the same.
