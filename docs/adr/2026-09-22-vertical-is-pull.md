# Vertical is pull, horizontal is substitution

## Context

The chord map needs an arrangement a beginner can read without being taught, and it must
survive a change of style.

## Decision

The tonic sits in the middle. Upward means more pull towards home, downward means further
away. Substitutes of the same function stand beside their chord.

The axis is continuous: the dominant seventh sits _above_ the plain dominant, the tonic with a
minor seventh _below_ the plain tonic, because that chord pulls down towards the subdominant.
The minor subdominant sits below the subdominant.

## Why

The player noticed that a dominant and its seventh belong on one line with the tonic, and that
observation fixed the whole layout: intensification of a function is movement along the same
axis, not an ornament hanging off the side. Once that is true, a scale's chord map can be read
as a ladder from calm to tense.

## Rejected

_Small satellites attached to each chord for its variants._ They implied that a seventh is a
different chord rather than the same chord under more tension, and they cluttered the map.

_Arranging chords by the circle of fifths._ Correct for voice leading and wrong for function:
subdominant and dominant are the most common pair in cadences and share no note at all, so no
geometry can put them both near the tonic and near each other.
